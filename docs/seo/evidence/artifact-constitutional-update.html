3. Judiciary, Judicial Independence and Judicial Review

Constitutional Law · Group 6 · 636 words · about 3 min read

  • What judicial independence protects
  • Judicial review
  • Pakistan: the 2025 judicial framework change

Official Syllabus Focus

  • Independence of judiciary
  • Judicial review
  • Theories of constitutional interpretation

Introduction and Scope

What this topic covers: The judiciary interprets law, resolves disputes and, in systems with judicial review, may examine whether public action conforms to the constitution.

What this topic covers: Judicial independence concerns institutional and decisional freedom from improper influence. Judicial review is the power to test legislation or executive action against higher constitutional law. Interpretation theories differ on how judges should use text, history, structure, purpose and precedent.

What judicial independence protects

Judicial independence exists to protect impartial adjudication. It has an institutional dimension, concerning the judiciary as a branch, and an individual dimension, concerning the ability of a judge to decide a case according to law without improper pressure. Independence is therefore compatible with lawful accountability; it does not mean that judges are free from constitutional limits or ethical obligations.

Common safeguards include rules on appointment, security of tenure, removal through defined procedures, financial security, court administration and protection from external interference. Their design differs across constitutional systems.

Judicial review

Judicial review is the authority to test legislative or executive action against higher constitutional law. It should be distinguished from an ordinary appeal. An appeal usually asks a higher court to reconsider a lower court decision in a case; constitutional review asks whether public action itself is legally compatible with the constitution.

The intensity and remedy of judicial review depend on the constitutional system. Some questions may involve direct invalidation, reading down, declarations, constitutional writs or other remedies. The existence of review therefore does not by itself answer how far courts may go.

Pakistan: the 2025 judicial framework change

The Twenty-seventh Amendment Act, 2025 introduced the Federal Constitutional Court. Section 21 inserted Articles 175B–175L; Articles 175E and 175F address its original and appellate jurisdiction. Section 29 omitted Article 184. For a current answer, distinguish this framework from earlier discussions of Supreme Court jurisdiction under Article 184(3), and read the High Courts’ Article 199 jurisdiction alongside the updated court provisions.

Source: Source checked 15 October 2026: official amendment Act, sections 19, 21 and 29. Check later changes against the National Assembly Constitution downloads. This study update does not replace the constitutional text.

Constitutional interpretation

Interpretive approaches include close attention to text, historical meaning, constitutional structure, precedent, purpose and underlying constitutional values. Real judicial reasoning often combines methods rather than following a single pure theory. The analytical task is to explain why a method is relevant to the legal question and how it constrains the interpretation.

Debates over judicial activism and restraint should be presented as debates about institutional role, democratic authority, rights protection and the limits of adjudication. They should not be reduced to praise or criticism of a particular political outcome.

Core Concepts Explained

  • Judicial independence has two dimensions: institutional independence of the judiciary as a branch and decisional independence of individual judges when deciding cases.
  • Safeguards commonly concern appointment, tenure, removal, remuneration, court administration and protection from improper pressure. Their purpose is impartial adjudication, not judicial isolation from all accountability.
  • Judicial review means examining whether legislation or executive action complies with higher constitutional norms. It is different from an appeal, which usually reviews a lower court decision within an ordinary case.
  • Constitutional interpretation asks how broad constitutional language should be understood. Textual, historical, structural, purposive and precedent-based approaches may point to different reasoning, and judges may combine them.
Common confusion to avoid: Do not describe judicial review as unlimited judicial supremacy. Its scope depends on the constitution, jurisdiction and remedial rules of the legal system.

Preparation and Answer Use

  • Learn institutional safeguards for judicial independence.
  • Separate judicial review from ordinary appeal.
  • Prepare interpretation approaches in neutral language: textual, historical/original, purposive, structural and precedent-based.

Key Takeaways

Key takeaway: Independence supports impartial adjudication.

Key takeaway: Judicial review enforces constitutional supremacy where the constitution permits it.

Key takeaway: Interpretation converts broad constitutional text into rules for cases.
