Published in 1962, Capitalism and Freedom argues that economic freedom is closely connected with political and personal liberty. Milton Friedman maintains that competitive markets allow individuals to choose where to work, what to buy, what to produce and how to use their property. Because economic decisions are spread among millions of people, markets can prevent excessive power from becoming concentrated in government. Friedman does not demand the total disappearance of the state. He accepts the need for government to maintain law and order, protect property, enforce contracts, provide stable money and address certain problems that markets cannot solve. Nevertheless, he believes government programmes frequently become inefficient, coercive or controlled by special interests. His preferred approach is therefore limited government, competition, voluntary exchange and individual responsibility. The book remains one of the most influential modern defences of classical liberalism and market-oriented public policy.

  1. Economic freedom is valuable in itself: People should generally have the freedom to choose their employment, business, purchases and use of personal property.
  1. Economic power should be decentralised: Competitive markets distribute decision-making among many individuals rather than placing it in the hands of one authority.
  1. Markets can limit political power: When people can earn income independently of the state, governments have less ability to punish them economically for their opinions.
  1. Voluntary exchange can benefit both sides: In a genuinely free transaction, buyers and sellers participate because each expects to gain from the exchange.
  1. Competition is essential: Friedman’s defence of capitalism depends on open competition. Monopoly, whether private or governmental, reduces choice and concentrates power.
  1. Government has limited but important functions: It should protect the country, enforce laws and contracts, preserve competition and provide a stable legal and monetary framework.
  1. School choice is one of his proposals: Friedman supports publicly financed education but argues that families should have greater freedom to choose schools through educational vouchers.
  1. He questions occupational licensing: Licensing may protect consumers in some cases, but Friedman argues that it can also protect established professionals from competition.
  1. Main lesson of the book: Political liberty is more secure when citizens possess economic independence and when neither government nor private monopolies control all opportunities.
  1. Study-note wording (edition unverified): “Economic freedom is also an indispensable means toward the achievement of political freedom.” Friedman presents economic choice as an important protection against political domination.
