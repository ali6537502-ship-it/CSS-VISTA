## Review boundaries

All 79 pages across the five supplied chapters were read and individually dispositioned. Private records map each page to lesson sections, cover/index status, corrections and excluded claims. This is a thematic educational adaptation with documented limits; it is **not** certification of every assertion in the original notes, independent human academic approval or full-library completion.

Sir Syed's chronology distinguishes the 1875 school opening from the 1877 college milestone using Graham's original account. The 1873 English *Causes of the Indian Revolt* identifies an 1858 Urdu composition; later catalogue wording is not silently substituted for that evidence. Original political speeches and correspondence, including Tyabji's different position, support comparison without inventing quotations or a unanimous Muslim political view. Unverified Patna/Gurdaspur quotations and conflicting service-entry years are not used as keyed facts.

Sirhindi's lifetime is distinguished from later reception. The course does not place his personal activity under rulers after his death or infer decisive court policy from the existence of letters. Competing memoir and devotional explanations remain distinct. Conflicting letter totals are not converted into a universal answer. Historical polemic is attributed and examined, rather than endorsed or erased.

Waliullah's course retains translation, Hadith study, qualified legal reasoning, social cooperation, economic transactions, fiscal pressures, public works and political correspondence. Selected passages in *Hujjat Allah al-Baligha*, consulted in Hermansen's translation, corroborate concrete mechanisms. They do not authenticate every modern welfare, banking or colonial-inheritance claim in the notes. The course corrects the reversed chronology concerning al-Shatibi and the simplified wujud/shuhud binary; Metcalf describes a reconciliatory argument. Intellectual transmission is not an inevitable chain leading to a modern state.

The Barelvi course separates Syed Ahmad of Rae Bareli from Ahmad Riza Khan of Bareilly and Sir Syed. Immediate military opponents and wider political aims are distinguished where historians differ. Sources disagree on 6 versus 8 May for Balakot; the course tests the shared 1831 event rather than a false consensus. Unverified final words, troop totals, burial accounts and modern-group claims stay outside the lessons.

The composite Sufism chapter retains four orders, the Faraizi movement, comparative Brahmo/Arya context and Deoband, Islamia College Peshawar, Anjuman Himayat-i-Islam and Sindh Madressatul Islam. Foundation records were checked against institutional sources. The course avoids a rigid spiritual-versus-scholarly binary and religious/ethnic stereotypes. Contested traditions are distinguished from authenticated events; historical analogy does not certify present law, incidents or policy success.
