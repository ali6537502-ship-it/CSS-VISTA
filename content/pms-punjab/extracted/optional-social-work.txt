                                                Topics

1   Origins, Evolution, Value Base and Philosophical Foundations

    1.1 Professional Ethics, Values and Social Work Theories

          Core Values and Ethics: Dignity and worth of the individual, social justice, integrity,
           service, competence, and professional boundaries (IFSW Ethical Principles).
          Operational     Principles:     Individualization,      acceptance,    self-determination,
           non-judgmental attitude, confidentiality, and controlled emotional involvement.
          Social Work Theories and Models: Psychodynamic Theory, Ecological Systems
           Theory, Cognitive-Behavioral Framework, Task-Centered Model, Deficits-based vs
           Strength-based perspectives, and Systems Approach in practice.

    1.2 Human Growth, Personality Development & Behavior

          Human Life-Cycle Stages: Physical, cognitive, and psycho-social development across
           the lifespan (Infancy, Childhood, Adolescence, Adulthood, Aging) based on theoretical
           perspectives including Bowlby’s attachment theory, Freud’s psychoanalytic theory,
           Erikson’s Psychosocial stages, and Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs in clinical casework,
           diagnosis, and intervention.
          Environmental Stressors: Impact of social inequality, trauma, family dysfunction, and
           environment on human behavior.

    1.3 Society, Social Institutions and Social Change

          Concept of society, rural-urban divide and its impact on human well-being
          Role of Social institutions including Family, Educational, Economic, Religious
           institutions and Peer interaction in individuals and communal well- being
          Concept of Social Change and its role in the development of human society

    1.4 Religious, Legal & Institutional Infrastructure

          Islamic Foundations: Huqooq-ul-Ibad, social justice, and the welfare state framework
           (Riyasat-e-Madina model); statutory Islamic redistribution mechanisms (Zakat, Ushr,
           Sadaqat, Khums, Waqf) for human well being in Islam.
          Constitutional & Legal Framework: Constitutional provisions for social security,
           human rights, labor protection, and social welfare in Pakistan.
          Institutional Delivery Systems: Role of Ministry of Human Rights, Provincial Social
           Welfare Departments (Punjab), Punjab Social Protection Authority (PSPA), and
           Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs) in welfare delivery.

2   Primary Methods of Direct Social Work Practice

    2.1 Social Case Work (Practice with Individuals & Families)

          Core Framework: Person, Problem, Place, professional and Process (the 5 Ps).
          The Clinical Process: Intake/Study, Social Diagnosis, Treatment/Intervention planning,
           Evaluation, and Termination. Referral mechanisms
          Therapeutic Relationship: Communication skills, diagnostic interviewing, transference,
           counter-transference, and home visiting.

    2.2 Social Group Work (Practice with Groups)

          Definition & Purpose: Therapeutic, developmental, educational, and task-oriented
           group work.
          Group Process & Dynamics: Group formation, stages of group development (forming,
           storming, norming, performing, adjourning), leadership models, program planning, and
           conflict resolution.
          Role of Group Worker: Enabler, facilitator, therapist, and resource mobilizer.

    2.3 Community Organization & Development (Practice with Communities)

          Practice Models: Locality Development, Social Planning, and Social Action Models
           (Rothman's Models).
          Community Work Process: Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA), Participatory Reflection
           and Action (PRA), community mobilization, committee formation, indigenous resource
           identification, and project implementation.
          Role of Community Organizer: Advocate, catalyst, planner, negotiator, and mediator.

3   Section 3: Secondary Methods, Policy, Administration & Research

    3.1 Social Welfare Administration, Policy & Planning

          Social Policy Formulation: Policy making process, social planning at provincial
           (Punjab) and federal levels, and policy analysis models.
          Administrative Processes: Translating social policy into action—POSDCORB
           (Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, Budgeting) or a
           Holistic Approach in welfare agencies.
          NGO Governance and Management: Registration and regulation under the Voluntary
           Social Welfare Agencies Ordinance 1961; Role of Punjab Charity Commission, Human
           Resource Development & Management (HRDM) in social sector; donor compliance,
           transparency, and project sustainability.

    3.2 Social Action, Human Rights and Policy Advocacy

          Methods of Systemic Change: Rights-based approaches, policy advocacy, lobbying,
           coalition building, awareness campaigns, and social movement dynamics.
          Civic Engagement and Human Rights: Civil society activism, protection of
           fundamental rights, and community empowerment frameworks.

    3.3 Social Work Research

          Research Methodology: Quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods research designs;
           diagnostic assessment vs. evaluative research.
          Data Collection & Analysis: Sampling techniques, survey design, Focus Group
           Discussions (FGDs), key informant interviews, social indicators, basic social statistics,
           and statistical data analysis in empirical research.

4   Socio-Cultural Environment

    Analyzing client environments and cultural systems to design responsive social work
    interventions.

    4.1 Pakistani Society & Cultural Context

          Sociological & Anthropological Foundations: Material and non-material culture;
           cultural values, norms, folkways, and mores unique to Pakistan.
          Social Stratification: Class, caste (biradari systems), gender hierarchies, status
           dynamics, and socio-economic dependencies in Pakistan.

    4.2 Social Institutions in Transition

          Family & Marriage: Dowry systems, arranged vs. autonomous marriages, domestic
           violence, divorce trends, early/child marriage and family breakdown as target areas for
           family counseling.
          Religious & Educational Systems: Mosques, Madrassas, and religious leaders as
           stakeholders in community mobilization; public-private educational divides and regional
           literacy gaps.

    4.3 Social Change & Global Development Frameworks

          Social Change Dynamics: Application of evolutionary, cyclical, and conflict theories to
           South Asian contexts; drivers (technology, globalization, mass media) and barriers
           (cultural lag, dogmatism, illiteracy).
          Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): Alignment of local social work practice with
           key UN SDGs (No Poverty, Good Health, Quality Education, Gender Equality and
           Reduced Inequalities).

5   Fields of Social Work Practice, Criminology & Emerging Challenges

    5.1 Public Health, Medical & Psychiatric Social Work

          Medical Social Work: Psycho-social assessments, hospital-based social work, patient
           counseling, rehabilitation, and managing welfare funds (e.g., Zakat committees in
           hospitals).
          Psychiatric Social Work: Mental health crisis intervention, substance abuse prevention,
           treatment, and rehabilitation.
          Public Health: community health education, especial in situations like COVID-19

     5.2 Criminology & Social Legislation

           Criminology & Deviance: Causes of crime, deviance vs. delinquency, juvenile
            delinquency, white-collar crime, drug trafficking, cybercrime, and gender-based
            violence.
           Correctional Social Work: Probation, parole, juvenile justice systems, prison reform,
            and social reintegration of offenders.
           Social Legislation in Pakistan: Legal provisions regarding child rights, women’s rights,
            domestic violence, labor welfare, anti-harassment laws, legislation on early/child
            marriage, anti-begging laws, and rights of persons with disabilities.

     5.3 Demographic & Vulnerable Population Interventions

           Child Protection & Youth Welfare: Prevention of child labor and abuse, child
            protection mechanisms (e.g., Punjab Child Protection & Welfare Bureau), adoption/legal
            custody, and youth development.
           Gerontological Social Work: Social, psychological, and physical aspects of aging;
            elderly care frameworks, institutional support, and community-based geriatric services.
           Gender & Vulnerable Groups: Gender Studies integration; crisis management centers
            (Dar-ul-Aman), economic empowerment of women, and specialized services for persons
            with disabilities (PWDs), transgender persons, faith based and ethnic minorities.

     5.4 Major Socio-Economic Problems & Climate Response

           Poverty & Safety Nets: Absolute, relative, multidimensional poverty, youth
            unemployment, hunger and malnutrition, social protection programs (BISP, Ehsaas,
            Punjab Social Protection Authority (PSPA)) and microfinance models.
           Climate Change & Environmental Social Work: Eco-social work practice; climate
            displacement, natural disaster response, disaster risk reduction (DRR), trauma counseling
            in emergencies, and building community resilience.
