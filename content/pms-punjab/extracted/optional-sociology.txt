                                           SOCIOLOGY
Total Marks: 100
                                                     Topics
    1   Introduction to Sociology and Core Sociological Concepts:
        • Meaning, nature, scope and historical development of Sociology as a discipline.
        • Sociology and common sense; sociology in relation to anthropology, psychology, economics,
        political science, and other social sciences.
        • Social groups, society, community, association, social institutions, social structure, social
        systems and social organization.
        • Status and role: ascribed status, achieved status, master status, role conflict and role strain.
        • Forms and processes of social interaction: cooperation, competition, conflict, accommodation
        and assimilation.
        • Social control, conformity and deviance as basic processes of social order.
    2   Culture, Socialization and the Development of Self:
        • Culture: material and non-material culture, ideal and real culture, norms, values, beliefs,
        symbols, language, folkways, mores, customs and laws.
        • Subculture, counterculture, ethnocentrism, xenocentrism, cultural relativism, cultural
        diffusion, acculturation and assimilation.
        • Primary and secondary socialization; resocialization and total institutions.
        • Agencies of socialization: family, school, peer groups, religion, media, workplace and digital
        platforms.
        • Charles Horton Cooley: looking-glass self.
        • George Herbert Mead: I and Me, significant others, generalized other, role-taking, play stage
        and game stage.
        • William F. Ogburn: cultural lag and the uneven pace of technological and normative change.
    3   Sociological Perspectives:
        • Structural functionalism: social order, integration, manifest and latent functions; Talcott
        Parsons and Robert K. Merton.
        • Conflict and neo-Marxist perspectives on power, inequality and institutions.
        • Symbolic interactionism: meaning, interaction, self and identity; Mead, Blumer and Goffman.
        • Feminist sociology: liberal, radical, Marxist/socialist, intersectional and postmodern
        approaches.
        • Exchange theory, phenomenology and ethnomethodology.
        • Modernism and postmodernism; selected ideas of Anthony Giddens, Pierre Bourdieu, Michel
        Foucault, and Jurgen Habermas.
    4   Classical and Contemporary Sociological Theory:
        • Ibn Khaldun; Asabiyyah (social solidarity), social change, rise and decline of civilizations,
        Nomadic and sedentary societies, political authority, economy, education, and historical
        method.
        • Al-Farabi; ideal society, political organization, social cooperation, leadership, morality, and
        social order.

    • Al-Ghazali; morality, social behavior, family, education, social responsibility, ethics,
    economic conduct, and community life.
    • Ibn Rushd; reason, law, politics, social organization, justice, and the relationship between
    religion and rational inquiry.
    • Auguste Comte: positivism, social statics, social dynamics and the Law of Three Stages.
    • Herbert Spencer: social evolution, organic analogy and militaristic/industrial societies.
    • Karl Marx: historical materialism, mode of production, class struggle, surplus value,
    exploitation and alienation.
    • Emile Durkheim: social facts, division of labour, mechanical and organic solidarity, anomie,
    suicide and religion.
    • Max Weber: social action, verstehen, ideal types, rationalization, bureaucracy, authority and
    class-status-party.
    • Georg Simmel: Formal sociology and forms of social interaction, sociation
    (Vergesellschaftung), dyad and triad, the stranger, Conflict as a Form of Social Interaction,
    Exchange, The Philosophy of Money, Objective Culture and Subjective Culture.
    • Phenomenology; critical theory; Pierre Bourdieu; Anthony Giddens; Michel Foucault;
    Zygmunt Bauman; intersectionality; postcolonial and decolonial thoughts.
