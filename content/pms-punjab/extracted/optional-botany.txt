                                    BOTANY
Total Marks: 100
                           SECTION A: CLASSICAL BOTANY
                                             Topics
  1.   Cryptogamic Botany:
       Algae: Classification (Fritsch, Bold & Wynne), Major algal groups (Cyanophyta,
       Chlorophyta, Charophyta, Bacillariophyta, Phaeophyta, Rhodophyta), thallus
       organization, pigments, reserve food, life cycles (Isomorphic, Heteromorphic,
       Diplontic, Triphasic). Economic importance (agar, alginate, biofertilizers). Algal
       blooms.
       Fungi:     Classification (Ainsworth), Major fungal groups (Phycomycetes,
       Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, Deuteromycetes), cell wall, hyphal modifications,
       reproduction. Lichens; mycorrhizae (Ectomycorrhizae vs Endomycorrhizae). Plant
       diseases & control.
       Bryophytes: Liverworts, Hornworts, Mosses – comparative study. Alternation of
       generations, apogamy, apospory.

  2.   Pteridophytes & Gymnosperms:
       Pteridophytes: Psilopsida, Lycopsida, Sphenopsida, Pteropsida. Heterospory & seed
       habit.
       Gymnosperms: Cycadales, Ginkgoales, Coniferales, and Gnetales– their structure,
       life history, affinities. Seed structure & ontogeny. Economic importance.
  3.   Plant Anatomy & Embryology:
       Anatomy: Meristems (apical meristems and the tunica-corpus theory). Tissue systems.
       Vascular cambium & secondary growth (dicot stem/root). Anomalous secondary
       growth. Leaf, stem, root anatomy.
       Embryology: Microsporogenesis, Megasporogenesis. Anther & ovule types. Pollination,
       fertilization, double fertilization. Embryo & endosperm development. Polyembryony,
       apomixis.
  4.   Taxonomy of Angiosperms:
       Systematics: Bentham & Hooker, Engler & Prantl, Takhtajan, Cronquist, APG.
       ICBN/ICN rules. Taxonomic hierarchy. Herbarium & botanical gardens.
       Modern     Trends: Biosystematics,      Chemotaxonomy,     Numerical Taxonomy,
       Molecular Taxonomy.
       Families: Fabaceae, Solanaceae, Poaceae, Asteraceae, Liliaceae–their diagnostic
       characters and economic importance.

5.   Plant Physiology & Biochemistry:
     Water relations: Water potential (Ψw = Ψs + Ψp + Ψg) and its components; water
     absorption; transpiration; and stomatal regulation. Phloem transport (Pressure-Flow
     Hypothesis).
     Mineral Nutrition: Macro/micronutrients,        deficiency   symptoms, active/passive
     transport.
     Photosynthesis a n d respiration: Light-dependent reactions (Z-scheme), Calvin
     cycle, C3/C4/CAM/pathways, photorespiration, and celluar respiration including
     glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, the electron transport chain (ETC), and oxidative
     phosphorylation.
     Plant hormones: Auxins, gibberellins acids (GAs), cytokinins, abscisic acid (ABA),
     and ethylene. Photoperiodism, Vernalization, Dormancy, Germination.


     SECTION B: APPLIED, MOLECULAR & ENVIRONMENTAL BOTANY

                                            Topics

6.   Ecology & Environmental Biology:
     Factors: Climatic (Light, Temperature, Wind), Edaphic (Soil), Biotic. Population &
     Community Ecology: Sampling (Quadrat, Line intercept), Ecological characteristics,
     Succession.
     Ecosystem ecology: Components, food chains, food webs, ecological
     pyramids, and biogeochemical cycles including t he hydrological (water) and the
     nitrogen cycle.
     Applied Ecology: Soil salinity & water logging in Pakistan – causes & reclamation.
     Deforestation, Biodiversity conservation, Pollution.
7.   Cytology & Genetics:
     Cytology: Cell cycle, Mitosis, Meiosis. Ultra-structure of organelles (Mitochondria,
     Golgi,     E R,    Plastids, Ribosomes, Nucleus). Chromosomal           aberrations;
     aneuploidy, euploidy, inversions, and translocations.
     Genetics: Mendelian laws of segregation and independent assortment; backcross and
     test crosses. Dominance, Incomplete dominance. Sex- linked inheritance. Linkage
     & Crossing over. Multiple alleles, Epistasis.
8.   Molecular Biology & Biotechnology:
     Molecular biology: DNA as the hereditary material; DNA replication; transcription; the
     genetic code; and translation. Regulation of gene expression (lac operon).
     Biotechnology: Genetic engineering, genetically modified organisms (GMOs), tissue
     culture, and applications in agriculture and medicine.
