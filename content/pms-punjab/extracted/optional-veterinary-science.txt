                                              Topics
  1.   APPLIED BASIC VETERINARY SCIENCES
       Applied anatomy and physiology: Functional organisation of the digestive,
       respiratory, cardiovascular, urinary, reproductive, endocrine, nervous and
       musculoskeletal systems; skin, udder and lactation. Relevant differences among
       ruminants, equines, companion animals and poultry, with applications to health and
       production. Applied biochemistry: Principles of nutrient metabolism, energy
       balance, fluid and electrolyte balance and acid–base regulation; their relevance to
       nutritional, metabolic and clinical disorders. General pathology: Cell injury,
       inflammation, degeneration, necrosis, circulatory disturbances, tissue repair and
       neoplasia; interpretation of these processes in common animal diseases. Necropsy
       and interpretation of common gross lesions. Microbiology, immunology and
       parasitology: Characteristics of bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites relevant to
       disease transmission and control. Host–pathogen relationships, innate and acquired
       immunity, maternal immunity, vaccination and immunosuppression. Application of
       parasite life cycles to prevention and control.
  2.   CLINICAL MEDICINE, DIAGNOSTICS AND PRIORITY ANIMAL
       DISEASES
       Clinical assessment: History taking, systematic clinical examination, identification
       of problems, differential diagnosis and clinical decision making. Recognition of
       emergencies. Diagnostic approaches: Selection, collection, preservation and
       transportation of specimens. Interpretation of basic hematology, serum biochemistry,
       urinalysis, fecal examination, cytology, microscopy, microbial culture, antimicrobial
       susceptibility reports, serology and molecular tests. Principles, indications and
       limitations of radiography and ultrasonography. Priority livestock infectious
       diseases: Foot-and-mouth disease, haemorrhagic septicemia, blackleg, anthrax,
       peste des petits ruminants, lumpy skin disease, bovine ephemeral fever, brucellosis,
       bovine tuberculosis and rabies. Priority poultry infectious diseases: Newcastle
       disease, avian influenza, infectious bursal disease, infectious bronchitis, Marek’s
       disease, salmonellosis and colibacillosis. Parasitic and vector-borne diseases: Tick
       infestations, theileriosis, babesiosis, anaplasmosis, trypanosomiasis, gastrointestinal
       helminthiasis, fasciolosis, coccidiosis, mange and other common ectoparasite
       infestations; practical control strategies. Common clinical and production
       disorders: Mastitis, neonatal diarrhoea, pneumonia, bloat, ruminal acidosis,
       displaced abomasum, hypocalcaemia, ketosis, pregnancy toxemia and lameness.
       Recognition, diagnostic approach, management principles and prevention of
       production losses. Representative fungal diseases: dermatophytosis and
       aspergillosis; recognition, diagnostic approach, management principles and
       prevention. Companion and working animals: canine parvovirus infection, canine
       distemper, feline panleukopenia, equine colic, strangles, glanders and equine
       piroplasmosis. Preventive healthcare of companion animals, working equids and
       camels.

3.   VETERINARY SURGERY, ANESTHESIA, REPRODUCTION AND
     OBSTETRICS
     Surgical principles: Animal handling and restraint; asepsis, sterilisation, wound
     assessment, wound healing and surgical infection prevention. Indications and
     welfare considerations for common procedures, including castration, dehorning,
     hernia repair and management of hoof conditions. Anesthesia and emergency care:
     Principles of local and general anesthesia, analgesia and perioperative monitoring.
     Initial assessment and stabilisation of shock, hemorrhage, traumatic injuries and
     fractures; postoperative care; principles of fluid and electrolyte therapy; recognition
     of dehydration and acid–base disturbances. Reproductive management: Estrous
     cycles, estrus detection, breeding timing, artificial insemination, semen quality and
     handling, breeding soundness examination of males, pregnancy diagnosis and
     reproductive records. Interpretation of conception rate, services per conception and
     calving interval. Infertility and reproductive disorders: Anestrus, repeat breeding,
     reproductive infections, abortion and common postpartum disorders; diagnostic
     approach, prevention and herd-level management. Obstetrics and neonatal care:
     Recognition and management principles of dystocia, retained fetal membranes,
     metritis and uterine or vaginal prolapse. Parturition management, colostrum
     provision and prevention of neonatal losses.

4.   EPIDEMIOLOGY, HERD HEALTH AND BIOSECURITY
     Epidemiological principles: Disease occurrence and distribution; incidence,
     prevalence, morbidity, mortality, case fatality and attack rate. Risk factors,
     association and causation; transmission patterns and population susceptibility.
     Research and evidence interpretation: Principles of sampling, observational
     studies and clinical trials. Basic descriptive statistics, bias, confounding and
     uncertainty. Interpretation of simple tables, graphs and study findings. Interpretation
     of relative risk, odds ratios and confidence intervals using simple supplied data;
     statistical significance and practical importance. Diagnostic performance:
     Sensitivity, specificity and predictive values; interpretation of screening and
     confirmatory tests. Surveillance and outbreak investigation: Passive, active and
     sentinel surveillance; case definitions, line lists, time–place–animal analysis,
     specimen selection, source tracing and disease reporting. Early warning, disease
     mapping and risk-based surveillance. Herd and flock health programs:
     Vaccination strategies, vaccine cold chains, assessment of vaccination failure,
     quarantine, isolation, cleaning, disinfection, movement management and farm
     biosecurity.

5.   VETERINARY PHARMACOLOGY, TOXICOLOGY AND
     ANTIMICROBIAL STEWARDSHIP
     Rational therapeutics: Applied pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics;
     selection of drug class, route and regimen according to clinical indication.
     Contraindications, interactions, adverse effects and assessment of treatment
     response. Principles of antimicrobial, antiparasitic, anti-inflammatory, analgesic and
     supportive therapy. Prescription quality, storage, product quality, treatment records
     and pharmacovigilance. Antimicrobial resistance and stewardship: Selection and
     transmission of resistance among animals, humans and the environment;
     consequences for animal health, public health and food production. Culture and
     susceptibility testing, empirical versus targeted treatment; antimicrobial selection
                                                                                    Page 2 of 7
     and duration; distinctions among treatment, metaphylaxis and prophylaxis.
     Protection of critically important antimicrobials and monitoring of antimicrobial
     use. Drug residue prevention: Withdrawal periods, maximum residue limits and
     prevention of veterinary-drug residues in milk, meat and eggs. Veterinary
     toxicology: Cyanide, nitrate or nitrite, urea, pesticides, mycotoxins, heavy metals
     and poisonous plants. Recognition, diagnostic approach, immediate response,
     principles of antidotal and supportive treatment and prevention.

6.   VETERINARY PUBLIC HEALTH, ONE HEALTH AND FOOD SAFETY
     One Health: Relationships among human, animal, plant and environmental health.
     Joint surveillance, information sharing, coordinated risk assessment and prevention
     at the livestock–wildlife–human interface. Institutional coordination, community
     participation and preparedness for zoonotic outbreaks. Priority zoonoses: Rabies,
     brucellosis, bovine tuberculosis, anthrax, leptospirosis, Q fever, salmonellosis,
     campylobacteriosis,      echinococcosis,   toxoplasmosis and Crimean–Congo
     haemorrhagic fever. Reservoirs, exposure routes, occupational risks and coordinated
     prevention.     Milk and animal-source food hygiene: Hygienic production,
     collection, processing, storage and transportation of milk, meat, eggs and fish.
     Pasteurization, cold-chain management and prevention of contamination and
     adulteration. Meat inspection and slaughter hygiene: Objectives and principles of
     ante-mortem and post-mortem inspection; slaughter hygiene, identification of
     public-health hazards and handling of condemned material. Food-safety
     management: Biological, chemical and physical hazards; antimicrobial residues,
     aflatoxins and other contaminants. Good hygiene practices, hazard analysis and
     critical control points (HACCP), traceability, sampling, recall and risk
     communication. Environmental and occupational safety: Safe handling of
     animals and specimens; personal protection, laboratory biosafety and biosecurity.
     Management of carcasses, manure, wastewater, sharps and veterinary waste.

7.   ANIMAL PRODUCTION, NUTRITION AND FARM MANAGEMENT
     Livestock production systems: Smallholder and commercial dairy, beef, sheep,
     goat and camel production systems. Locally important breeds, production
     constraints and opportunities for improvement. Housing and husbandry: Housing,
     ventilation, stocking density, water supply, sanitation and animal comfort. Calf and
     young-stock rearing, milking management, lactation and dry-period management;
     maintenance of working animals. Poultry production: Broiler, layer and breeder
     management; hatchery hygiene, incubation, hatchability, chick quality, brooding,
     lighting, litter management and ventilation. Performance assessment: Milk yield
     and quality, growth, mortality, culling, reproductive efficiency, feed conversion, egg
     production and flock uniformity. Use of farm records and benchmarks to identify
     and resolve management problems. Nutritional principles: Functions of energy,
     protein, fibre, minerals, vitamins and water; digestion and nutrient utilisation in
     ruminants, monogastric animals and poultry. Balanced feeding: Requirements for
     maintenance, growth, reproduction and production; body-condition assessment and
     recognition of nutritional deficiencies and excesses. Simple ration evaluation using
     supplied feed-composition and requirement data. Fodder and feed resources:
     Locally available feeds, pasture management, seasonal fodder planning, hay, silage,
     crop residues and safe use of non-conventional feeds. Feeding management:
     Feeding of young, transition and lactating animals and poultry; prevention of
     feeding-related disorders, drought feeding and water security. Feed safety and
                                                                                    Page 3 of 7
      efficiency: Sampling, quality assessment, adulteration, spoilage, mycotoxins and
      storage losses. Safe use of supplements, urea and additives; principles of probiotics,
      prebiotics and feed enzymes. Reduction of feeding costs and nutrient losses while
      maintaining health and productivity. Aquaculture: Basic production systems, water
      quality, stocking, feeding, biosecurity and recognition of fish mortality events; food-
      safety implications and integrated production systems. Wildlife and zoo animals:
      Principles of conservation, captive-animal health, quarantine, welfare and disease
      risks at wildlife–livestock interfaces.

8.    ANIMAL GENETICS, BREEDING AND BIOTECHNOLOGY
      Genetic principles: Inheritance, quantitative traits, heritability, selection, breeding
      values, inbreeding, crossbreeding and heterosis. Breeding programs: Performance
      and pedigree recording; selection of economically important traits. Breeding
      strategies suited to local production conditions; conservation of indigenous breeds,
      adaptability and disease resistance. Reproductive biotechnology: Estrus
      synchronization, semen cryopreservation, sexed semen, embryo transfer and in vitro
      embryo production. Diagnostic biotechnology: Applications and limitations of
      conventional polymerase chain reaction (PCR), reverse-transcription PCR, real-time
      PCR, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), rapid antigen tests, point-of-
      care diagnostics and genomic sequencing. Vaccines and emerging technologies:
      Principles and applications of conventional, recombinant, viral-vectored and
      nucleic-acid vaccines. Genomic selection and its practical applications. Awareness
      of emerging applications of gene editing, nanotechnology and targeted drug
      delivery.
9.    DIGITAL VETERINARY SERVICES, ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND
      PRECISION FARMING
      Digital services: Electronic clinical and farm records, animal identification,
      traceability, mobile disease reporting, geographic information systems (GIS) and
      tele-veterinary services. Precision livestock farming: Sensors and wearables for
      monitoring activity, estrus, rumination, temperature, milk quality and farm
      conditions. Artificial intelligence: Basic concepts of artificial intelligence (AI) and
      machine learning. Applications in diagnostic-image interpretation, disease
      surveillance, outbreak-risk prediction, production forecasting and clinical decision
      support. Generative AI applications in documentation, information retrieval and
      farmer communication; verification of outputs, factual errors and limitations of
      automated advice.
10.   LIVESTOCK ECONOMICS, POLICY, GOVERNANCE AND EXTENSION
      Sector development: Contributions of livestock and poultry to livelihoods,
      nutrition, food security, employment and rural development; principal constraints
      and development priorities.
      Applied economics: Fixed and variable costs, farm budgets, gross margins, break-
      even analysis and partial budgeting. Simple cost–benefit and cost-effectiveness
      comparisons; economic losses from animal disease. Markets and value chains:
      Livestock and product marketing, quality incentives, producer organisations, credit,
      insurance, traceability and barriers to domestic and export market access. Policy
      and regulation: Objectives and implementation of frameworks concerning animal
      disease control, quarantine, veterinary medicines, feed quality, breeding, food safety
      and welfare. Veterinary certification and principles of sanitary trade requirements.

        administration, laboratories, universities, professional regulators, private providers
        and international organisations. Veterinary service delivery and extension: Access
        in underserved areas, mobile services, referral networks, farmer education,
        participatory extension, public–private partnerships and entrepreneurship. Inclusion
        of women and smallholders; risk communication and management of
        misinformation.
        Program management and professional ethics: Needs assessment, priority setting,
        objectives, budgeting, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Accountability,

        public vaccination campaigns; procurement and distribution of veterinary supplies,
        maintenance of essential stocks and equitable allocation of veterinary services.
  11.   ANIMAL WELFARE, CLIMATE RESILIENCE AND DISASTER
        MANAGEMENT
        Welfare assessment: Physical and behavioural indicators; nutrition, environment,
        health, behaviour and mental state. Prevention of avoidable pain, injury, fear and
        distress. Humane management: Welfare during housing, handling, transport,
        markets and slaughter; euthanasia principles. Welfare of companion, working,
        laboratory and captive wildlife animals; responsible ownership and humane
        population management. Ethical animal use: Principles of replacement, reduction
        and refinement in teaching and research. Climate resilience: Effects of heat stress,
        floods, drought and changing vector distributions on health and productivity.
        Sustainable production: Efficient resource use, manure management, reduction of
        greenhouse-gas emissions and nutrient losses, biodiversity conservation and
        sustainable pasture management. Disaster management: Risk assessment,
        contingency planning, evacuation, rescue, emergency shelter, feed and water
        provision, carcass disposal and continuity of veterinary services. Post-disaster
        surveillance, rehabilitation and coordination with disaster-management authorities.
