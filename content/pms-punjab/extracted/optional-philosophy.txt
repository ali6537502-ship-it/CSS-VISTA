                                       PHILOSOPHY
Total Marks: 100

                                                 Topics

1    INTRODUCTION TO PHILOSOPHY
        Definition, nature, value and scope of philosophy
        Philosophical Methods: Socratic Method (Socrates), Inductive Method (Bacon, Mill), Deductive
         Method (Aristotle, Descartes), Dialectical Method (Hegel), and Fallibilistic Method (Popper)

2    ONTOLOGICAL FOUNDATIONS:
       Objective Idealism: The World of Forms, Allegory of the Cave (Plato)
       Transcendental Idealism: Phenomena vs. Noumena, A Priori Forms of Sensibility, Synthetic A
        Priori Knowledge (Immanuel Kant)
       Absolute Idealism: The Absolute (Geist/Spirit), Dialectical Method. The Real is Rational,
       (G. W. F. Hegel)

3    EPISTEMOLOGICAL APPROACHES:
       Rationalism: Knowledge through pure reason, innate ideas, and logical deduction (Descartes,
         Spinoza, Leibniz)
       Empiricism: Knowledge derived through sensory experience, tabula rasa, observation, and
         induction (Locke, Berkeley, Hume)
       Skepticism: Limits of knowledge, radical doubt, justification, and certainty (Academic vs.
         Pyrrhonian Scepticism; Hume's Scepticism)

4    LOGIC AND SCIENTIFIC METHOD
       Definition: Its rules, types and purposes
       Deduction vs. Induction: Understanding Truth and Validity
       Informal Fallacies: 1. Fallacies of Ambiguity: Equivocation, amphiboly. Accent, Composition,
        Division. 2. Fallacies of Presumption: Accident. Complex Question, Circular Fallacy (Petitio
        Principii), 3. Fallacies of Relevance: Appeal to Force, The Appeal to Populace, Appeal to
        Emotion, The Red Herring, The Straw Man, Attack on the Person, Missing the Point
       Scientific Explanation & Methodology: Scientific vs. unscientific explanations, formulation,
        classification, and verification / falsification of hypotheses

5    ETHICAL THEORIES
       Character-based Ethics / Virtue Ethics: Nicomachean Ethics, Eudaimonia, Doctrine of the Mean
        (Aristotle)
       Duty-based Ethics / Deontology: Categorical Imperative, Goodwill, Duty (Immanuel Kant)
       Result-based Ethics / Utilitarianism: Quantitative Hedonism (Jermey Bentham), Qualitative
        Hedonism (J. S. Mill)

6    PHILOSOPHICAL MOVEMENTS
       Logical Positivism: Verification Principle & Refutation of Metaphysics (A.J. Ayer)
       Dialectical Materialism: Laws & dynamics of dialectics, Theory of Knowledge (Marx)
       Existentialism: Existence precedes essence, Radical Freedom and Responsibility, Bad Faith,
        Anguish, Being-for-Itself vs. Being-in-Itself (Sartre)
       Postmodernism: Deconstruction, Logocentrism, Aporia, Différance (Derrida)

7      MUSLIM THEOLOGY & SUFISM
         Mutazilites: Five core theological principles (al-usul al-khamsa)
         Asharites: Divine Attributes (Sifat), Uncreatedness of the Quran, Free Will and Acquisition
          (Kasb)
         Sufism: Its origin, definition and core characteristics, Pantheism / wahdat-al-wajud (Ibn Arabi),
          Panentheism / wahdat-al-shuhud(Sheikh Ahmmad Sirhindi)

8      MUSLIM THINKERS
         Al-Farabi: Theory of the Intellect, Theory of Ideal State (al-Madīna al-Fādila)
         Ibn Sina: Theory of Knowledge, Systematic psychology / Mind / Body relationship
         Al-Ghazali: Skeptical method, Refutation of Philosophers (Tahafut al-Falasifah)
         Ibn Rushd (Averroes): Reconciliation between Philosophy and Religion, Way to knowledge
         Ibn Khaldun: Philosophy of History, Theory of Social Cohesion ('Asabiyyah), Critique of
          Metaphysics
         Shah Waliullah: Metaphysics, Concept of Reconciliation (Tatbiq), Social and Economic
          Philosophy (Irtifaqat)
         Muhammad Iqbal: Concept of Ego (Khudi), Ijtihad as the Principle of Movement in Islam
