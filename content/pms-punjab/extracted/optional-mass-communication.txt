Total Marks: 100

PART-I:       FOUNDATIONS OF MASS COMMUNICATION, COMMUNICATION
              THEORY AND MEDIA–SOCIETY RELATIONS
                                         Topics
Introduction to Mass Communication; Communication Theories; Advanced Theories of
Communication; Media and Society; Media and Information Literacy.
  • Process and models of communication. Source–message–channel–receiver–noise–
    feedback and redundancy; types and levels (intrapersonal, interpersonal, group,
    organisational, mass); Shannon–Weaver, Lasswell, Schramm, Osgood, Berlo, Westley–
    MacLean, Gerbner and Dance models; movement from linear to transactional and
    networked models.
  • Barriers and principles of effective communication. Physical, semantic, psychological,
    cultural and technological barriers; principles of effective communication (clarity,
    completeness, conciseness, credibility, context, courtesy, feedback).
  • Functions and nature of mass media. Surveillance, interpretation, socialisation,
    entertainment and mobilisation; characteristics of mass audiences; mass communication
    and mass self-communication.
  • Media effects and audience theories. Hypodermic needle/magic bullet model; two-step
    flow of communication and opinion leaders; limited-effects paradigm; agenda-setting,
    framing and priming; gatekeeping; spiral of silence; cultivation; uses and gratifications;
    media dependency; social learning; active and participatory audiences.
  • Normative and critical perspectives. Four Theories of the Press and their critiques;
    social-responsibility, development-media and democratic-participant theories; public
    sphere; Frankfurt School and the culture industry; hegemony; political economy of
    media; encoding/decoding; media imperialism; feminist and postcolonial critiques.
  • Media, culture and society. Public opinion and propaganda; representation,
    stereotyping, gender and minorities; popular culture and consumerism; media and
    democracy; mediatisation of society.
  • Media and information literacy. Access, analysis, evaluation and creation of media
    content; misinformation, disinformation and malinformation; propaganda techniques;
    fact-checking and verification; critical media consumption.

  PART-II: MEDIA IN PAKISTAN: HISTORY, STRUCTURE, LAWS AND ETHICS
                                    Topics
Mass Media in Pakistan; Media Ethics & Laws in Pakistan; Introduction to Mass
Communication.
 • Press of the Sub-Continent. Origins of the Urdu and English press; the role of Sir Syed
   Ahmed Khan, Maulana Zafar Ali Khan, Muhammad Ali Jauhar, Hasrat Mohani and
   Hameed Nizami; the press and the Pakistan Movement.
 • Evolution of media in Pakistan. Press after 1947; radio (Radio Pakistan, FM and
   community radio); television (PTV and the expansion of private satellite channels after
   2002); internet and social media; ownership patterns and media groups.
 • Organisational structures of newspapers and news agencies. Editorial, news, production,
   circulation, advertising and management departments; APNS, CPNE and journalists’
   unions; national and international news agencies.
 • Government–media relationship. Censorship, control and patronage; government
   advertising; media under different political regimes; press freedom, pressure on journalists
   and safety of media professionals.
  • Press laws and regulation. Articles 19 and 19A of the Constitution; Registration of
    Printing Presses and Publications Ordinance 1988; Press Council of Pakistan Ordinance
    2002; PEMRA Ordinance 2002; Defamation Ordinance 2002; contempt of court; Right of
    Access to Information Act 2017; Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016;
    Protection of Journalists and Media Professionals Act 2021 (all as amended).
  • Ethics of journalism and freedom of the press. Accuracy, objectivity, fairness, privacy
    and public interest; codes of conduct (PEMRA and professional bodies); sensationalism,
    media trials, conflict of interest and sting operations; ethical challenges of digital and
    social platforms.
  • Social and developmental responsibilities of Pakistani media. Public-service role in
    education, health, gender equity, national integration, minorities, disaster reporting and
    democratic processes.
  • Contemporary challenges. Economic pressures, digital disruption, political and
    commercial influence, polarisation and disinformation.

PART-III:    JOURNALISM: NEWS              REPORTING,         WRITING,       EDITING      AND
              PRODUCTION
                                          Topics
Fundamentals of News Reporting; News Production & Current Affairs; Sub-Editing & Page
Designing; Op-ed Writing; Data Journalism; Documentary Production.
  • News. Definition and news values; sources; structure (inverted pyramid and alternative
    forms); language and style; types of news and beats.
  • Reporting skills. Newsgathering, interviewing, attribution and verification; political,
    court, crime and investigative reporting.
  • Opinion and interpretive genres. Feature, column, editorial and op-ed: differences in

    objectives, structure, style and content.
  • Sub-editing and page design. Copy editing, headline and caption writing, house style,
    layout principles, typography, photographs and infographics for print and digital pages.
  • Broadcast journalism and production. Radio and television news writing; bulletin and
    current-affairs production; importance of radio and TV documentaries and live
    programmes; differences between print and electronic-media news.
  • Digital and data-driven journalism. Online, multimedia and mobile journalism; social-
    media newsgathering and verification; citizen journalism; data journalism and
    visualisation.



PART-IV: ADVERTISING, PUBLIC RELATIONS AND MEDIA ECONOMICS
                                       Topics
Introduction to Advertising & Public Relations; Media Enterprise Management; Media
Advocacy.
  • Advertising: concepts and functions. Definition, nature and types; advertising appeals;
    basics of consumer behaviour; merits and demerits (economic, social and ethical).
  • Advertising business in Pakistan. Growth and structure of the industry; local and
    multinational agencies; departments of an advertising agency (client service, creative,
    media planning and buying, research, production, finance); regulation, self-regulation and
    ethical issues.
  • Branding, integrated marketing communication and media planning. Brand building;
    IMC; campaign planning; reach, frequency, GRPs and CPM; digital and influencer
    advertising.
  • Advertising as the lifeblood of media. Revenue models of newspapers, broadcasters and
    digital platforms; ratings and audience measurement; advertiser influence and editorial
    independence.
  • Public relations: definition, scope and tools. PR process (research, planning,
    implementation, evaluation); media relations, press releases, events, publications and
    digital tools; PR in Pakistan (government, corporate and non-profit sectors).
  • PR, propaganda, advertising and publicity. Conceptual differences and overlaps;
    persuasion and ethics; corporate communication, reputation and CSR.
  • Importance of research in advertising and PR. Audience and market research; copy
    testing; campaign evaluation; public-opinion surveys; communication audits.
  • Media enterprise management. Organisational structure, leadership, financial planning,
    resource management, entrepreneurship and innovation in media businesses.

PART-V: DEVELOPMENT, CONFLICT AND INTERNATIONAL COMMUNICATION
                                    Topics
Development Support Communication; Conflict & Crisis Communication; International
Communications; Media Advocacy.
 • Development communication and development journalism. Concepts and paradigms
   (modernisation, dependency, participatory and multiplicity approaches); diffusion of
   innovations; critique of development journalism.
 • Conceptual distinctions. Mass communication, development communication,
   development journalism and development support communication: purposes, content,
   audiences and practice.
 • Communication for social change. Behaviour-change communication in health,
   education, environment and climate, gender and disaster risk reduction; Sustainable
   Development Goals; campaign design and evaluation; community and rural media; ICT for
   development.
 • Media advocacy. Strategies for influencing public opinion and policy; digital activism;
   evaluating advocacy campaigns.
 • Conflict and crisis communication. Media role in conflict, war and terrorism; war versus
   peace journalism; conflict-sensitive reporting; propaganda in conflict; crisis and disaster
   communication (phases, stakeholders, strategies).
 • International communication. Global information flows; NWICO and the MacBride
   Report; international news agencies and broadcasters; media imperialism and cultural
   globalisation; soft power and public diplomacy; comparative media systems; international
   media institutions and policy.
PART-VI:       DIGITAL MEDIA,    EMERGING                         TECHNOLOGIES              AND
               COMMUNICATION RESEARCH
                                        Topics
Introduction to Digital Media; AI & Data Analytics in Media; Communication Research
Methods; Advanced Research Methodologies.
 • Digital media. Evolution of the web and digital platforms; convergence; participatory
   culture; the network society; content creation, distribution and audience engagement.
 • Social media and society. Algorithmic curation, echo chambers and polarisation; digital
   activism; hate speech and content moderation; platform governance; privacy, surveillance
   and digital rights in Pakistan.
 • Artificial intelligence, data and media. Generative AI in content production; automated
   and personalised news; deepfakes and synthetic media; algorithmic bias; data analytics and
   audience measurement; ethical, copyright and regulatory issues.
  • Communication research. Research process; problem statement, literature review,
    theoretical framework, variables and hypotheses; quantitative methods (survey,
    experiment, content analysis) and qualitative methods (interview, focus group, case
    study, textual and discourse analysis); mixed methods; sampling; reliability and validity;
    descriptive and basic inferential statistics; research ethics; proposal writing and APA (7th
    ed.) referencing.
