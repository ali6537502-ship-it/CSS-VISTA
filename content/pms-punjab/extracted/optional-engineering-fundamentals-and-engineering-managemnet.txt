             ENGINEERING FUNDAMENTALS AND ENGINEERING
                           MANAGEMENT
Total Marks: 100

                     PART A:        ENGINEERING FUNDAMENTALS
1.   APPLIED PHYSICS:
     A. Measurement and mathematical description: SI units, dimensions and dimensional
     consistency; scalars and vectors; significant figures; measurement uncertainty and
     interpretation of graphs and experimental data.
     B. Classical mechanics and waves: Density; gravity; position, velocity and
     acceleration; mass, momentum, force and energy; angular momentum and torque; work
     and power; conservation laws, oscillations and engineering applications.
     C. Quantum mechanics and particle physics: Planck’s constant, quanta and
     quantization; matrix and wave mechanics at an introductory level; quantum harmonic
     oscillator; quantum numbers and spin; wave function; wave-particle duality; zero-point
     energy; Pauli’s exclusion principle and Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle; fundamental
     forces; elementary particles, antimatter, quantum gravity and vacuum energy at
     conceptual level.
     D. Relativity: Principle of relativity; inertial frames and invariance; speed of light;
     length contraction and time dilation; rest mass and rest energy; gravity, general relativity
     and stress-energy tensor at conceptual level.
     E. Optics: Ray and wave optics; reflection, refraction and scattering; lenses, mirrors and
     prisms; diffraction, dispersion and polarization; aberrations, optical resolution and
     optical instruments.
     F. Solid-state physics: Amorphous and crystalline solids; crystal structures; free-
     electron model; valence and conduction bands, band gap, electron and hole excitation;
     superconductivity and crystal optics.

     Suggested readings:
        1. Hugh D. Young and Roger A. Freedman, Sears and Zemansky's University
           Physics with Modern Physics.
        2. Karl F. Kuhn, Basic Physics.
        3. David Halliday, Robert Resnick and Jearl Walker, Fundamentals of Physics.
        4. Douglas C. Giancoli, Physics for Scientists and Engineers with Modern Physics.
2.    APPLIED CHEMISTRY:
      A. Chemical foundations: Atomic structure, periodic trends, chemical bonding,
      stoichiometry and concentration calculations; acids, bases, pH and buffers.
      B. Gases, liquids and solutions: Boyle's law and Charles's law; gas constant; Dalton's
      partial-pressure law; Graham's diffusion law; kinetic theory and heat capacity of gases;
      vapour pressure and boiling point; surface tension, viscosity, refractive index,
      polarimetry, intermolecular and intramolecular forces; ideal and non-ideal solutions,
      Raoult's law and Henry's law, solubility curves and heat of solution.
      C. Solid state and X-rays: Crystalline structure and properties; production of X-rays and
      crystal-structure applications; heat capacities of solids.
      D. Electrochemistry: Electrolysis; electrolytic conductance and migration of ions;
      galvanic, reversible and irreversible cells; standard electrode potentials; electrochemical
      series, buffer solutions and engineering applications.
      E. Thermochemistry and kinetics: Heat of reaction, basic chemical equilibrium and
      factors affecting reaction rate.
      F. Industrial chemistry: Corrosion mechanisms and control; water hardness and
      treatment; fuels and combustion; lubricants; cement chemistry; environmental chemistry,
      chemical hazards and safety.
      G. Basic analytical methods: Titrimetry, spectrophotometry and chromatography:
      principles and interpretation at introductory level.

     Suggested readings:

       1.   P. C. Jain and Monica Jain, Engineering Chemistry.
       2.   Shashi Chawla, A Textbook of Engineering Chemistry.
       3.   S. S. Dara and S. S. Umare, A Textbook of Engineering Chemistry.
       4.   B. K. Sharma, Engineering Chemistry.


3.    APPLIED ELECTRICITY AND ELECTRONICS:
      A. Circuit fundamentals: Electrical potential; resistance, conductance, conductivity and
      impedance; Ohm's law; series and parallel circuits; practical resistors; work, power,
      energy and Joule's law; capacitance and capacitors in series/parallel; AC quantities.
      Network solution methods: Kirchhoff's laws; nodal and mesh analysis; source
      transformation; Thevenin, Norton and maximum-power-transfer theorems.
      B. Electricity and magnetism: Electric-field intensity and Gauss's theorem; capacitors,
      capacitance; capacitors in series and parallel; force on a current-carrying conductor;
      electric and magnetic circuits and leakage flux; Faraday's and Lenz's laws; induced
      emf/current; self and mutual inductance and series/parallel combinations; hysteresis;
      magnetic-field energy; generation of alternating voltage/current.
      C. Machines and transformers: DC shunt, series, and compound motors; speed-torque
      relations; single- and three-phase induction and synchronous motors; generators;
      transformers; principle, construction, ratios, connections, losses, performance and
      efficiency.
     D. Electronics and digital systems: n- and p-type materials; PN junctions and diodes;
     transistor types and basic circuit calculations; Class A/B and feedback amplifiers; RC
     oscillators; op-amps, timers, flip-flops, converters and filters.
     Digital logic and embedded systems: Number systems, Boolean logic,
     combinational/sequential circuits, microcontrollers and embedded-system concepts.
     E. Communication, signals and control: EM theory, antennas, gain, free-space loss
     and fading; AM, FM, PM, PWM, delta modulation, FSK, ASK and PSK; error
     correction, demodulation, detectors, transmitters and receivers; filters, Z-transform, S-
     domain, transfer functions, Nyquist criterion and stability.
     F. Computing: Computer evolution; CPU, storage, input/output and multimedia;
     operating systems and programming languages; networks and internet; systems analysis
     and design; MIS and e-commerce; security, privacy and professional ethics.
     G. Power systems: Network analysis; polyphase circuits; transients; transmission lines
     and losses.
     Protection, measurement and modern power: Electrical measurements, sensors and
     transducers; protection and earthing; electrical safety; power factor; introductory power
     electronics, renewable-energy integration and smart grids.

     Suggested readings:

         1. B. L. Theraja and A. K. Theraja, A Textbook of Electrical Technology.
         2. B. P. Lathi and Zhi Ding, Modern Digital and Analog Communication Systems.
         3. William Stallings, Wireless Communications and Networks.
         4. Charles K. Alexander and Matthew N. O. Sadiku, Fundamentals of Electric
            Circuits.
         5. Stephen J. Chapman, Electric Machinery Fundamentals.
         6. Thomas L. Floyd, Electronic Devices.
         7. M. Morris Mano and Michael D. Ciletti, Digital Design.


4.   MECHANICAL ENGINEERING FUNDAMENTALS:
      A. Engineering mechanics and strength of materials: Force systems, equilibrium,
      friction, centroid and geometric properties; motion, work and energy; stress and strain;
      engineering and true stress/strain; elastic and plastic deformation; tensile testing and
      stress-strain curve; yield and ultimate strength; elongation, toughness, resilience,
      ductility and malleability; shear, bending, torsion, bending moment and principal
      stresses; Brinell and Rockwell hardness.
      B. Fluid mechanics and machines: Fluid properties; statics and dynamics; laminar
      and turbulent flow and Reynolds number; continuity, momentum and energy
      concepts; head loss; power transmission by fluids; pumps and turbines.
      C. Thermodynamics and heat transfer: Properties of fluids and steam; first and
      second laws; enthalpy, entropy and heat capacity; Carnot cycle; Gibbs free energy and
      equilibrium; PV diagrams; heat engines; two and four-stroke heat engines; steam
      turbines and power plants; refrigeration and air conditioning; conduction, convection
      and radiation; evaporators and condensers.
        D. Manufacturing: Casting, forging, rolling, extrusion, wire drawing, welding,
        machining, turning, milling, shaping, gear cutting, drilling and fitting.
        E. Machine and production practices: Basic machine elements and design
        considerations; metrology, limits, fits, tolerances and surface finish; engineering
        drawing and CAD/CAM, CNC and additive manufacturing concepts; instrumentation,
        condition monitoring, maintenance and workshop safety.
     Suggested readings:
     1. J. L. Meriam and L. G. Kraige, Engineering Mechanics: Statics.
     2. William A. Nash, Schaum's Outline of Strength of Materials.
     3. Serope Kalpakjian and Steven R. Schmid, Manufacturing Engineering and
        Technology.
     4. R. C. Hibbeler, Mechanics of Materials.
     5. Yunus A. Cengel and Michael A. Boles, Thermodynamics: An Engineering
        Approach.
     6. Yunus A. Cengel and John M. Cimbala, Fluid Mechanics: Fundamentals and
        Applications.
     7. R. S. Khurmi and J. K. Gupta, A Textbook of Machine Design.

5.   MATERIALS ENGINEERING :
     A. Structure of materials: Classification of materials; atomic structure and metallic
     bonding; crystal structures and geometry.
     Crystal defects and diffusion: Point, line and surface defects; diffusion mechanisms and
     their engineering significance.
     B. Metallic materials: Mechanical properties of metals and alloys; ferrous and non-
     ferrous metals and alloys; industrial applications.
     Phase relations and heat treatment: Binary phase diagrams; iron-carbon system; phase
     transformations; annealing, normalizing, hardening and tempering.
     C. Polymers, ceramics and glasses: Polymerization; general-purpose and engineering
     thermoplastics, thermosets and rubbers; traditional and engineering ceramics; ceramic
     processing and electrical, mechanical and thermal properties; glasses.
     D. Composites and advanced materials: Particle-, fibre-, and laminate-reinforced
     composites; introductory nanomaterials, biomaterials and functional materials.
     E. Behaviour, testing and selection: Tensile, hardness, impact and fatigue concepts;
     fracture, creep, corrosion and wear; introductory optical and electron microscopy, and X-
     ray characterization; material selection based on performance, cost, safety and
     sustainability.
     Suggested readings:
     1. William F. Smith and Javad Hashemi, Foundations of Materials Science and
        Engineering.
     2. Sidney H. Avner, Introduction to Physical Metallurgy.
     3. William D. Callister Jr. and David G. Rethwisch, Materials Science and
        Engineering: An Introduction.
     4. Michael F. Ashby, Materials Selection in Mechanical Design.
     5. D. R. H. Jones and Michael F. Ashby, Engineering Materials
6.   CIVIL ENGINEERING FUNDAMENTALS:
      A. Structures: Stress, strain, shearing force and bending moment; simply supported and
     cantilever beams; columns and footings; basic structural behaviour; pulleys and gears as
     elementary mechanics applications.
      B. Civil engineering materials and construction: Properties and uses of cement,
     aggregates, concrete, steel, bitumen and masonry; basic construction methods, estimation
     and quality control.
      C. Surveying and geomatics: Distance, angle and level measurement; traversing,
     contours and setting out; introductory total station, GNSS and GIS concepts.
       D. Geotechnical engineering: Soil classification, compaction, consolidation,
     permeability, shear strength, bearing capacity, settlement, soil improvement, and slope
     stability and its mitigation at an introductory level.
     E. Transportation engineering: Highway administration and scheme preparation; traffic
     and environmental appraisal; highway geometry, drainage, lighting, signing,
     communications and safety; urban roads and traffic; pavement materials and basics;
     maintenance and low-cost roads in developing countries.
     F. Environmental engineering: Environmental impacts of water-resource and
     transportation projects; water supply and distribution; wastewater treatment and
     management.
     Solid waste, pollution, and assessment: Municipal solid-waste management; air, noise
     and smog pollution basics; environmental-impact assessment and mitigation.
     G. Hydraulics and water-resources engineering: Fluid properties; pressure- and flow-
     measuring devices; pipeline losses; open channels; barrages and dams.
     Hydrology and resilience: Hydrologic cycle, rainfall-runoff and flood concepts; climate-
     resilient infrastructure, disaster-risk awareness and occupational safety.

     Suggested readings:
        1. A. M. Neville, Properties of Concrete.
        2. T. P. Kanetkar, Surveying and Levelling.
        3. E. John Finnemore and Joseph B. Franzini, Fluid Mechanics with Engineering
           Applications.
        4. M. S. Shetty, Concrete Technology: Theory and Practice.
        5. B. C. Punmia, Ashok Kumar Jain and Arun Kumar Jain, Surveying.
        6. K. R. Arora, Soil Mechanics and Foundation Engineering.
        7. L. R. Kadiyali, Traffic Engineering and Transport Planning.
        8. S. K. Garg, Water Supply Engineering; and Sewage Disposal and Air Pollution
           Engineering.
                PART B: ENGINEERING MANAGEMENT
1.   ENGINEERING MANAGEMENT:

     Engineering Economy, Cost and Financial Management
     Cost classification and estimation; time value of money; simple and compound interest;
     cash-flow diagrams; present and future worth; Net Present Value (NPV)and Net Future
     Value (NFV); annual worth; internal rate of return; benefit-cost ratio; break-even
     analysis; budgeting; basic income statement and balance sheet; depreciation, inflation,
     replacement, life-cycle costing, financial risk and sensitivity analysis.
     Project, Risk and Professional Management
     Project objectives and life cycle; scope and work-breakdown structure; timelines,
     milestones, dependencies and Gantt charts; CPM and PERT; resource and cost
     estimation; resource allocation and levelling; progress monitoring; project risk
     identification, assessment, response and control; procurement, contracts, stakeholders,
     communication, change control and closure.
     Professional practices and work ethics: management functions; teamwork and
     leadership; engineering ethics; public and environmental responsibility; occupational
     health and safety; technical communication and accountability.
     Quality, reliability, and testing: Quality planning, assurance, control and improvement;
     Deming, Juran and Crosby principles; quality circles; management responsibility;
     purchasing and design validation; test plans, execution, records and reports; destructive
     and non-destructive testing; inspection, sampling, acceptance criteria, calibration and
     measurement uncertainty; quality audit and corrective and preventive action.
     Reliability and improvement tools: PDCA; basic control charts and process capability;
     root-cause analysis; ISO 9001 concepts; failure modes; reliability, availability,
     maintainability, verification, validation and traceability.
     Operations, Production, Inventory and Decision Tools:
     Production and operations management; manufacturing systems; process selection and
     optimization; time analysis and work study; resource and raw-material planning;
     capacity, scheduling and production control; inventory identification and systems; FIFO
     and LIFO as issue and valuation methods; problem analysis, decision-support systems,
     decision trees, design of experiments and management tools.
     Essential operations tools: ABC analysis, EOQ, reorder point, lead time and safety
     stock; MRP and JIT concepts; method study, process charts, work measurement and
     standard time; facility layout, productivity, lean principles, 5S, maintenance, safety and
     waste minimization; SWOT, Pareto and cause-and-effect analysis.

     Suggested readings:
        1. Dennis Lock, Handbook of Engineering Management.
        2. O. P. Khanna, Industrial Engineering and Management.
        3. T. R. Banga and S. C. Sharma, Industrial Engineering and Management Science.
        4. R. Panneerselvam, Engineering Economics and Management.
        5. Jay Heizer, Barry Render and Chuck Munson, Operations Management:
           Sustainability and Supply Chain Management.



Note:   The above list is only suggestive and not exhaustive. Candidates may consult other
