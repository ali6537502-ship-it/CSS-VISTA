                                        ZOOLOGY

Total Marks: 100
                                                TOPICS

1.   ANIMAL DIVERSITY (INVERTEBRATES)
     Introduction, taxonomy, general organization, structure and function, reproduction and life
     cycles, phylogenetic relationships, and economic importance of major invertebrate groups:
     Protozoa and Parazoa:
     Protozoa: Locomotion and types of pseudopodia, reproduction and parasitic protozoans.
     Porifera: Cellular organization, body wall, skeleton, and canal systems.
     Radiata, Triploblastic Acoelomates, and Pseudocoelomates:
     Cnidaria: Body wall, nematocysts, polymorphism, alternation of generations and coral reefs.
     Platyhelminthes: Body wall, parasitic adaptations, and life cycles of some major groups
     (e.g., Fasciola hepatica, Taeniasolium).
     Aschelminthes and Nematoda:Pseudocoelomate organization, body wall, and life cycles of
     human parasitic nematodes (e.g., Hookworms, Pinworms, filarial worms).
     Coelomate Invertebrates and Major Lineages:
     Mollusca: Modifications of foot, feeding mechanisms and predatory adaptations of
     cephalopods.
     Annelida &Arthropoda: Metamerism, tagmatization, mouthparts, ecdysis, metamorphosis,
     and ecological importance.
     Echinodermata: Diversity; water vascular system, larval forms and development.

2.   Animal Diversity (Vertebrates)
     Chordata: Introduction and General Organization
     Origin, diagnostic characteristics and basic chordate body plan; major subdivisions of
     chordates, including proto-chordates and vertebrates.
     Fishes: Vertebrate Success in Water
     Origin, diversity, classification and phylogenetic relationships of major fish groups.
     Structural and functional adaptations for aquatic life: swim bladder; respiration; excretion
     and osmoregulation.
     Amphibians: The First Terrestrial Vertebrates
     Origin, classification and evolutionary relationships of amphibians. Structural, physiological
     and behavioral adaptations for aquatic and terrestrial life: Respiration, reproduction and
     development.
     Reptiles: The First Amniotes
     Evolution of reptiles, adaptations for terrestrial life, excretion, osmoregulation, reproduction
     and development; Extinction of dinosaurs; poisonous apparatus and biting mechanism of
     snakes.

     Aves: Feathers, Flight and Endothermy
     Evolutionary history and major adaptations associated with feathers, flight and endothermy.
     Structural and functional adaptations for aerial locomotion and respiration. Bird migration
     and navigation.
     Mammalia: Specialized Teeth, Hair, Endothermy and Viviparity
     Origin, diversity and emphasis on major mammalian groups. Dentition in vertebrates.
     Comparative account of the evolution of the heart and vertebrate stomach. Temperature
     regulation and endothermy.

3.   PRINCIPLES OF LIFE AND GENERAL ZOOLOGY
     Cell and Molecular Biology
     Comparative analysis of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. Structure and specialized functions
     of the nucleus, plasma membrane and cytoplasmic organelles. Enzyme structure, catalytic
     mechanisms, regulation, and inhibition. Major metabolic pathways: glycolysis, the Krebs
     cycle, and the electron transport chain. DNA as genetic material, structural organization of
     nucleic acids, the genetic code, transcription, and translation. Mitosis and meiosis, and their
     biological significance.
     Animal Form, Function and General Physiology
     Vertebrate integument. Ultra-structure of muscle fibrils and mechanisms of muscle
     contraction. Regional functional organization of the gastrointestinal tract. Composition and
     functions of blood and internal fluids. Cardiovascular dynamics: electrical activity of the
     heart, cardiac output and blood pressure regulation. Comparative account of respiratory
     organs; properties and functions of respiratory pigments. Functional anatomy of the
     vertebrate nephron and urine formation. Mechanism of osmoregulation, and
     thermoregulation. Structural and functional units of the nervous system. Nerve impulse and
     its transmission. Structure and function of sensory organs (eye and ear). Endocrine glands,
     classes of hormones, mechanisms of hormone action.
     Genetics & Recombinant DNA Technology
     Mendelian principles of inheritance, gene interactions, multiple alleles, linkage, crossing
     over and sex-linked inheritance. Eukaryotic chromosome structure; chromosomal
     aberrations, gene mutations, and the evolving gene concept. Recombinant DNA technology,
     cloning strategies, applications of genetic engineering, and transgenic animals.
     Evolutionary Biology
     Theories of the origin of life. Darwinism and Neo-Darwinism. Causes of variation and types
     of natural selection. Genetic drift and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. Species concepts,
     mechanisms of speciation (allopatric, parapatric, sympatric), adaptive radiation, phylogeny
     and ontogeny.
     Environmental Biology & Ecology
     Ecosystem concepts: components, primary/secondary productivity, trophic levels, food
     chains, food webs, and energy flow dynamics. Biogeochemical cycles (carbon, nitrogen).
     Environmental pollution (air, water, land), greenhouse effect, acid rain, global warming,
     climate change, and wildlife conservation and management.
